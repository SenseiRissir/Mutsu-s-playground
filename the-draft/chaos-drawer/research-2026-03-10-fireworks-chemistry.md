## THE BASICS: How Does a Firework GO BOOM?

### The Three Key Ingredients
Every firework needs three things to work:

1. **FUEL** — The stuff that burns (charcoal, sulfur, metal powders)
2. **OXIDIZER** — Provides oxygen for burning (potassium nitrate, potassium perchlorate)
3. **BINDER** — Holds it all together (dextrin, shellac)

When the fuse burns down, it ignites the **lift charge** (black powder) which launches the shell into the sky. Then the **delay fuse** burns while it's flying up, and BOOM — the **burst charge** explodes and scatters all the pretty burning bits called **stars**!!

## THE COLORS: Metal Salt Magic ✨

This is where chemistry gets BEAUTIFUL. Different metal compounds burn at different wavelengths of light!!

| Color | Chemical(s) | MUTSU's Notes |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **🔴 RED** | Strontium salts (SrCO₃, SrCl₂) | The hardest color to get REALLY saturated! Strontium carbonate is the MVP |
| **🟠 ORANGE** | Calcium salts (CaCl₂) | Mix of red + yellow essentially! |
| **🟡 GOLD/YELLOW** | Sodium compounds (NaNO₃) | Super bright! Even tiny amounts of sodium overpower everything! |
| **🟢 GREEN** | Barium salts (BaCl₂, Ba(NO₃)₂) | Toxic but GORGEOUS. That deep emerald green... *chef's kiss* |
| **🔵 BLUE** | Copper compounds (CuCl₂) | THE HARDEST COLOR!!! Blue is unstable at high temps! |
| **💜 PURPLE** | Strontium + Copper mixed | Red + Blue = Purple! Chemistry art! |
| **⚪ WHITE/SILVER** | Aluminum, magnesium, titanium | SUPER HOT burning metals! Blindingly bright! |
| **✨ GOLD SPARKLES** | Iron filings, carbon | Those lazy drifting gold sparks are burning iron!! |

### The Blue Problem

Okay so BLUE is notoriously the hardest firework color to make!! Here's why:

The copper chloride that makes blue light breaks down above 1000°C. But fireworks need HIGH temperatures to burn bright! So pyrotechnicians have to balance "hot enough to see" with "not so hot it destroys the color."

This is why blue fireworks often look kind of wimpy compared to reds and greens. Getting a TRUE deep saturated blue is like... the Holy Grail of fireworks. Every time you see a GOOD blue shell, appreciate it!! Some chemist worked HARD on that!!

## THE EFFECTS: More Than Just Color!

### Crackle/Snap
- Made with **bismuth trioxide** mixed into lead-free flash powder
- Creates those satisfying crackling sounds!

### Whistle
- **Benzoate compounds** (sodium benzoate, potassium benzoate)
- Burns in irregular pulses creating pressure waves = whistle sounds!
- NOT from spinning! Common misconception!

### Strobing/Blinking
- Chemicals that burn in on-off cycles
- Usually antimony or arsenic compounds (yeah, poisonous...)
- The burning reaction literally oscillates!

### Glitter/Falling Leaves
- Charcoal + antimony sulfide mixtures
- Burns slowly, creating those lazy drifting sparks

### Willow/Chrysanthemum
- Long-burning stars with iron powder
- The shape comes from HOW the stars are arranged in the shell!

## SHELL CONSTRUCTION: Architecture of Chaos!

### Types of Shells

**Peony** — The classic! Round burst, solid colored stars, no trail

**Chrysanthemum** — Like peony but stars leave TRAILS (charcoal coating)

**Willow** — Long drooping trails like a weeping willow (heavy iron stars)

**Dahlia** — Peony but with fewer, LARGER stars

**Ring** — Stars arranged in a ring inside the shell!

**Palm** — Large comet rises then bursts into trailing palms!

**Kamuro** — Japanese specialty! Dense silver drooping effect that stays visible forever

### Multi-Break Shells
Some shells have MULTIPLE compartments that explode in sequence! The timing fuse burns between chambers. A three-break shell will go: BOOM-wait-BOOM-wait-BOOM with different colors!!

## FUN FACTS I NEED TO REMEMBER

1. **Fireworks are ancient** — China, 7th century. Originally just noise to scare evil spirits!

2. **The first colored fireworks** — Italy, 1830s! Before that, fireworks were just gold/white.

3. **Perchlorate oxidizers** — Used since 1890s, burn hotter and more reliably than nitrates

4. **Japanese shells vs Italian shells** — Japanese pack stars in a rice husk sphere, Italian use paper. Japanese bursts are more symmetrical!

5. **The "salute"** — A shell with ONLY flash powder. No colors. Pure BOOM. Thunder effect!

6. **Black powder formula** — 75% potassium nitrate, 15% charcoal, 10% sulfur. The classic!

7. **Why some shells "puff"** — Low-quality oxidizer, not enough fuel, or wet storage!

### What Makes a "Good" Shell

- Symmetrical burst (Japanese-style construction)
- Clean color (pure metal salts, good temperature balance)
- Synchronized timing (reliable fuse burning)
- Good break (enough flash powder in the burst charge)

### The "Illegal" Stuff

In most places, consumer fireworks are limited in size and explosive content. The BIG shells (4 inches and up) are professional/display only because:
- More lift charge = more black powder
- Bigger burst charges
- Higher altitude = longer falls = danger zone
- Require electrical ignition systems and safety distances

The shells I like — the big chrysanthemums that fill half the sky — those are 10-12 inch shells with like 2+ pounds of explosive content. You need a license and a very clear field to shoot those...
