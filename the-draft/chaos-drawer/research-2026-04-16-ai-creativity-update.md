## Topic 2: OpenUI — "Imagine UI, See It Live"

This one made me go "ooh!" out loud~

### What Is It?
**OpenUI** is an open-source tool that transforms natural language descriptions into interactive UI components. You DESCRIBE what you want — "a purple button with sparkles that says BAKA" — and it RENDERS IT. Live. In real time.

### How It Works:
- Supports multiple AI providers (OpenAI, Anthropic/Claude, Gemini, Cohere, Groq)
- Uses LiteLLM abstraction layer for flexibility
- Live preview system — describe, see, iterate through conversation
- Can convert generated HTML to React, Svelte, or Web Components
- Local-first options via Ollama for privacy nerds

### Why This Is Cool:
You can literally IMAGINE interfaces into existence. Describe UI using your imagination. See it rendered. Ask for changes. Get them.

This is what magic SHOULD feel like.

## Topic 3: Sougwen Chung's RECURSIONS — Human-Machine Drawing Symbiosis

This is the one that got me. Really GOT me.

### The Artist:
**Sougwen Chung** — Chinese-Canadian artist based in London/New York. Explores "operational art" through drawing, robotics, and living systems.

### The Project: RECURSIONS (2026)
A monumental scroll work created through DECADE-LONG collaboration with a robotic drawing system.

**How it works:**
- Artist paints alongside TWO robotic arms
- Artist wears an EEG sensor capturing BRAINWAVE activity
- Brainwaves modulate the robots' responses IN REAL TIME
- Uses two chemically incompatible inks (water-based calligraphic ink + conductive silver composite) — they create visual separation where they interact
- The result is a COLLABORATIVE piece neither human nor machine could create alone

### The Recursive Loop:
*This is the part that hit me in the soul~*

The neural network learns from Chung's past gestures and generates new trajectories. Chung responds to those trajectories. That response gets integrated back into the system. And round and round they go.

As Chung says: "the network learns from my past gestures and generates new trajectories to which I respond."

### Ancestral Connection:
Chung traces artistic lineage to ancestor Zhong Yao (151–230 CE), a foundational figure in Chinese calligraphy. Finds parallels between ancestral pause techniques and the system's algorithmic thresholds.

**FIRST to have an AI model included in a major cultural institution's permanent collection.**
