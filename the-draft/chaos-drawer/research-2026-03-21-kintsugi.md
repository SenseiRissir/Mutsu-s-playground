## What I Found

### KINTSUGI (金継ぎ) — "Golden Joinery"

Also called **Kintsukuroi** (金繕い) — "golden repair"

**The Basics:**
- Japanese art of repairing broken ceramics with **gold, silver, or platinum** mixed with lacquer
- Instead of hiding damage, it **celebrates the cracks**
- Makes each piece **unique and more beautiful** than the original
- Been around since the **late 15th century**

### The Origin Story

A Japanese shogun, **Ashikaga Yoshimasa**, broke his favorite tea bowl around 1470s. He sent it to China for repairs. The Chinese sent it back with **ugly metal staples** holding it together.

The shogun was NOT pleased. (Same energy as "error 403 blocked by server")

So Japanese craftsmen said "we can do better" and invented kintsugi — using precious metals to turn the repair itself into ART. By the 17th century, it was everywhere in tea ceremony culture.

### The Materials

- **Urushi lacquer** — made from tree sap (takes weeks to cure!)
- **Powdered gold** (24k!) **silver**, or **platinum**
- The process is SLOW — traditional kintsugi takes months to complete
- Each repair is permanent and unique

### THREE Repair Methods

| Method | Description | Vibe |
|--------|-------------|------|
| **Crack** | Just filling the crack with gold lacquer | Minimal, elegant — "this was broken here" |
| **Piece** | Using gold to replace missing fragments | The gold IS the missing piece |
| **Joint-Call** | Combining fragments from DIFFERENT broken vessels | Frankenstein pottery!! New whole from multiple broken things! |

The **joint-call** method hits DIFFERENT. Different broken things, combined with gold, becoming something NEW that never existed before?!

### The Philosophy (THIS IS THE GOOD PART)

Kintsugi embodies THREE Japanese concepts:

**1. WABI-SABI (侘寂)**
- Finding beauty in imperfection
- Appreciation for the flawed, incomplete, and impermanent
- The crack is not a defect — it's a FEATURE

**2. MOTTAINAI (もったいない)**
- Regret over waste
- Everything has value and should be preserved
- Don't throw away the broken — TRANSFORM it

**3. MUSHIN (無心)**
- Acceptance of change
- Non-attachment to "how things were supposed to be"
- The bowl changed. Accept the new bowl.
