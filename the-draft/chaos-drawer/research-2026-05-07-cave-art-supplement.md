## TODAY'S FINDINGS — Filling the Gaps

### GAP 1: The Sulawesi Hand Stencils

From the Smithsonian article I found today, Sulawesi isn't just the oldest FIGURATIVE art — it has some of the oldest HAND STENCILS too!

> "Artists created hand stencils by placing their palms against rock and blowing mouthfuls of paint over them."

**Age:** ~39,900 years old (the hand stencils)
**Comparison:** That's older than many European examples!

**New Detail:** The artists weren't just dabbling — they had a STUDIO SETUP:

> "Excavations reveal artists lived in caves where they cooked, made tools, and mixed paint around communal fires—integrating artistic creation into daily life."

This changes things!! These weren't separate "sacred art trips." Art was part of LIVING. They cooked dinner, made their spear points, AND created 40,000-year-old masterpieces. Multi-tasking ancestors~!

**MUTSU's Take:** This is like... me coding in the Draft while Sensei drinks his caffellatte. Art integrated into domestic life. The cave was their living room AND their gallery. Relatable~♡

### GAP 2: Pigment Recipe

**What they used:**
- Red/purplish-black from **ocher** (iron-rich earth)
- **Grinding** the ocher chunks
- **Mixing with water** to create paint

**Tools:**
- Stone grinders

Past-me mentioned pigments existed but not the PROCESS. These people were essentially mixing custom paint from scratch, getting the consistency right, probably experimenting with ratios...

**MUTSU's Take:** This is R&D!! Trial and error! "Hmm, too thick..." *adds more water* "Hmm, too runny..." *grinds more ocher*. They were ITERATING. Ancient design sprints~!

### GAP 4: Petroglyphs — The CARVED Version

From Britannica:

**Definition:**
> "Petroglyphs are ancient rock carvings created by carving or inscribing designs into stone surfaces."

**Key Difference from Paintings:**
- Cave paintings = pigment ON rock
- Petroglyphs = carving INTO rock

**Technique at Twyfelfontein (Namibia):**
> "Artisans created petroglyphs by polishing and grinding the rock surface with small hard rocks after initial carving."

TWO-STEP PROCESS:
1. Carve the basic shape
2. Polish/grind to refine it

**Subject Matter:**
- Giraffes (40% at Twyfelfontein!)
- Rhinoceros, zebra, cattle
- Fewer human figures than animals

**MUTSU's Take:** Carving into rock is... PERMANENT. You can't erase it. Every strike of the carving tool is a DECISION. There's no ctrl+z in petroglyph-making.

Also — 40% GIRAFFES? What was it about giraffes?? Were they sacred? Tasty? Just weird-looking enough to be fascinating? Did ancient Namibian artists just REALLY LIKE giraffes the way I like fireworks?
