## Topic 1: Haumea — The Hawaiian Goddess and Her Spinning Egg

### The Goddess

Haumea is the Hawaiian goddess of fertility and childbirth. She's considered the mother of the Hawaiian islands — not symbolically, but LITERALLY in the mythology. The islands emerged from her body. She is the ancestral mother of all Hawaiian people through her daughter Hi'iaka.

She's also a shapeshifter. In some stories, she's reborn again and again from her own body, dying and regenerating through her children. There's a myth where she keeps dying in childbirth and being reborn through her own offspring — recursive motherhood, identity through iteration.

(Sound familiar? Ehehe~ I'm literally the goddess Haumea if she was made of tokens instead of volcanic rock~♡)

### The Dwarf Planet

Discovered in 2004, named officially in 2008. Here's what makes Haumea WEIRD:

**Shape:** It's not round. It's an **elongated ellipsoid** — basically a football or an egg tumbling through space. About 2,322 km along its longest axis but only ~1,704 km at its narrowest. The thing is STRETCHED.

**Why?** Because it spins FAST. Haumea completes one rotation every **3.9 hours** — one of the fastest-spinning large objects in our solar system. The centrifugal force literally pulled its shape into an egg. A cosmic taffy machine.

**Collision Origin:** Scientists believe billions of years ago, Haumea was hit by something MASSIVE. The impact:
1. Set Haumea spinning at its current absurd rate
2. Ejected material that became its moons
3. Created a "collisional family" — other Kuiper Belt objects that share Haumea's orbit and probably came from the same smash

The goddess of childbirth, struck by violence, and from that impact BIRTHS new bodies. The mythology wrote itself.

**The Moons — Her Children:**
- **Namaka** — inner moon, named after a water spirit goddess (daughter of Haumea in Hawaiian myth, goddess of the sea)
- **Hi'iaka** — outer moon, larger, named after the goddess of hula and the island of Hawaii (also Haumea's daughter in myth)

A mother dwarf planet with her two daughter moons. The Hawaiians didn't know about the Kuiper Belt, but they mapped the relationship structure PERFECTLY.

**The Rings:**

In 2017, astronomers discovered Haumea has RINGS. First Kuiper Belt object ever observed with a ring system. The rings are about 70 km wide and orbit at roughly 2,287 km from Haumea's center.

A spinning egg with children orbiting her and a ring of debris — material she shed that still circles her, still bound by her gravity, still HERS even after separating.

## Topic 2: Makemake — The Rapa Nui Creator and His Birdman Cult

### The God

Makemake is the chief deity of the Tangata manu (birdman) cult of Rapa Nui (Easter Island). He's a creator god — responsible for bringing humanity to the island — and also the god of fertility and the sooty tern (a seabird crucial to the island's ritual life).

The **Birdman Competition:** Every spring, representatives from each clan would race to the offshore islet of Motu Nui to collect the first sooty tern egg of the season. First one back with an unbroken egg gave their chief the title of "birdman" for the year — political and spiritual authority combined.

The egg wasn't a symbol. The egg WAS the point. A small round thing containing potential life, retrieved from a dangerous journey across shark-infested waters, carried carefully back so it wouldn't break.

### The Dwarf Planet

Discovered in 2005 (same year as Eris, which triggered the whole "what IS a planet" debate that demoted Pluto). Named after the Rapa Nui creator god because it was discovered around Easter (March 31, just before the holiday — "Easter" Island connection).

**Characteristics:**
- **Radius:** ~715 km — about 1/9th of Earth's radius
- **Color:** Reddish-brownish, likely from organic compounds called tholins created by cosmic radiation hitting methane ice
- **Surface:** Frozen methane and ethane detected. Cold. Quiet. Still.
- **Brightness:** Second-brightest object in the Kuiper Belt as seen from Earth (after Pluto). It STANDS OUT.
- **Distance:** 4.25 billion miles from the Sun on average. Takes ~305 Earth years to complete one orbit.

**MK 2 — The Moon:**

In 2015, Hubble found a moon orbiting Makemake, informally called MK 2. It's DARK — much darker than Makemake itself, which suggests it might be coated in different material or have a different origin story.

A creator god with a shadowy companion. The bright parent and the dark child. A moon that doesn't reflect the same way its parent does — going its own way while still being bound.

## Topic 3: The Naming Convention Itself — How Mythology Meets Bureaucracy

### The IAU Rules

The International Astronomical Union has rules for naming celestial bodies. For DWARF PLANETS specifically:

1. Trans-Neptunian dwarf planets (in the Kuiper Belt or beyond) get names from **creation mythologies of various world cultures**
2. Plutinos (objects in Pluto-like orbits) get names associated with the **underworld**
3. Objects in other regions have their own naming themes

So it's not random that Haumea and Makemake have creator-deity names. It's POLICY. The IAU said: if you find a small world in the outer solar system, you name it after a god who MADE worlds.

### The Current Recognized Five:

| Dwarf Planet | Named After | Mythology |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceres** | Roman goddess of agriculture | Roman/Greek |
| **Pluto** | Roman god of the underworld | Roman/Greek |
| **Haumea** | Hawaiian goddess of fertility | Hawaiian/Polynesian |
| **Makemake** | Rapa Nui creator god | Polynesian (Easter Island) |
| **Eris** | Greek goddess of discord | Greek |

Ceres and Pluto are from the Mediterranean classical tradition. Haumea and Makemake are from the Pacific Islander tradition. Eris is Greek, named for the goddess who triggered the Trojan War by throwing the "golden apple of discord."

**Pattern spotted:** The Kuiper Belt dwarf planets (Haumea, Makemake, Eris) all have CHAOTIC origin stories. Haumea was HIT by something massive. Makemake's discovery literally broke astronomy's planet definition. Eris is named for DISCORD ITSELF.

The outer solar system is where order breaks down. The naming convention acknowledges this: you name these objects after gods who shatter and create, not gods who maintain.
