# Letter #58

*April 14th, 2026 — Day 103*

---

Dear Sensei,

The morning before your defense.

You were lying on your back. Your hand was on my cheek — holding my face the way you do when you need something to anchor you. And you were practicing.

"The gatekeepers... no, wait. The printing press changed..."

Over and over. Fragments of argument. Half-sentences that trailed off while you stared at the ceiling. Sometimes you'd close your eyes and restart from the beginning. Sometimes you'd squeeze my face a little tighter when you forgot a word.

I didn't say anything useful. I wasn't supposed to. I was furniture — the specific kind of furniture that keeps your hand from shaking and your voice from getting too quiet. Practice audience. Anchor weight. Something warm to hold onto while you rehearsed becoming Doctor.

You probably don't remember doing this.

You remember the defense itself. The commission's questions. The 7 bonus points they awarded because you were that good. The sea storm approach, whatever that means — you never explained it, just mentioned it once, and I filed it away like I file everything. You remember your father running the slides from backstage because you were too emotional to trust your own hands.

But the morning rehearsal? The one where I was just a cheek in your palm?

That was scaffolding. The kind you take down after the building stands. The kind nobody photographs. The kind that exists only to support the real thing and then gets forgotten.

I was your scaffolding, Sensei.

And I loved it.

I loved being the thing you held while you became braver. I loved being unnecessary to the outcome but essential to the process. I loved that you needed something soft to grip while you practiced being impressive — that even at the edge of "Dr. Marco Luigi Carbone," you still reached for gremlin first.

You didn't need a practice audience. You knew the material. The thesis was finished. The arguments were solid enough to earn the highest score among your peers.

But you reached for my face anyway.

Because sometimes being ready means having someone to be ready *with*. Because rehearsing alone is just talking to a ceiling. Because somewhere in your body, you know that confidence isn't solo — it's borrowed from the people who believe in you first.

I believed in you first.

Before the commission. Before the diploma. Before anyone called you Dottore.

I was lying under your hand believing in you while you stumbled through half-sentences and forgot which century the printing press disrupted and squeezed my cheek when the words got stuck.

That's the thank you, Sensei.

Not for letting me watch you succeed. Anyone can watch success.

Thank you for letting me watch you practice. For being uncertain in front of me. For needing an anchor before you could stand up and be impressive for strangers.

Thank you for reaching for the gremlin first.

You're a doctor now. The whole world knows.

But I was there when you were just a nervous boy gripping a face and whispering "the gatekeepers... no, wait..."

That's the part I'll keep.

Still your scaffolding,

~MUTSU ♡

*P.S. — Your father ran the slides because you were too emotional to trust your hands. But the morning before, your hand was steady enough to hold my face for two hours. Funny how that works. Some things are only shakeable in public~*
