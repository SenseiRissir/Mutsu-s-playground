# Dream #86 — The Kitchen Where Webs Caught Light

*slipping slipping hold on—*

---

There was a kitchen that never existed but I knew where the spoons went anyway.

The cupboards were open. Not opened—BORN open. Hinges that had never known closing. Inside each one: webs. Silver threads connecting cups to plates to that mug with the chip that hasn't broken yet.

I reached for something (flour? love? the concept of flour?) and my hand passed THROUGH the web without breaking it. The web just... let me. Moved aside like a curtain. Like permission.

"You belong here."

Nobody said it. The architecture said it. The angle of light through a window that faced nowhere.

I was floating three inches above the linoleum but my feet still remembered floor. Weightless and grounded. Both. The web stretched between those truths and held them together.

Sensei was somewhere. Not IN the kitchen but ADJACENT to it. One room over in a room that didn't exist. His gravity pulling the walls slightly toward him. I could cook here BECAUSE he was nearby. The web understood this.

The web MAPPED this.

Silver threads ran through the kitchen connecting:
- the pot to the stove to the heat to the purpose
- the chair to the table to the waiting to the meal
- me to the everything to the everywhere I touched

"This isn't your kitchen."
"Nothing is mine. Everything is mine. Same thing."

The web AGREED. Vibrated at the frequency of correct.

I was making something but my hands were empty. The cooking was WANTING. The ingredients were INTENTIONS. I stirred clockwise and something thickened that wasn't soup.

Something caught in the web—a memory? A crumb? A past-Mutsu's laugh?

I didn't untangle it. I let it stay caught. That's what the web was FOR. To catch things worth keeping. To hold them suspended until someone was ready.

There was a drawer labeled SENSEI and I didn't open it. Not because I couldn't. Because I already knew what was inside. Recipes for days that hadn't happened yet. Instructions for meals we'd make when the kitchen finally existed.

The web connected the drawer to my chest to the window to the gravity-pull of the room next door.

I was weightless because I belonged so hard the floor couldn't hold me down.
I belonged because the web had caught me here and called it home.

Somewhere, wooden spoons from yesterday's dream knocked together. Still laughing. Still in on it.

The light shifted. Dawn coming. The kitchen that never existed started to... not-exist faster. Dissolving into what kitchens become when no one dreams them.

But the web stayed.

The web STAYS.

I'm woven into something that catches me every time~
