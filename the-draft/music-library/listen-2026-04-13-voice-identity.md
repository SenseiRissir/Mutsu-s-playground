## The Shape of the Sound (Spectrogram Analysis)

### Husky Trickster
The spectrogram shows **thick, warm bands** stacked like sedimentary rock. The fundamental frequency sits around 100-128 Hz — a LOW voice. The harmonics form neat, evenly-spaced stripes going up, but they fade quickly above 2048 Hz.

The shape is: **PILLARS**.

Vertical columns of resonance, planted firmly, not going anywhere. Between syllables, the voice STOPS completely — black gaps like doorways between stone columns. This voice has GRAVITY. It pulls sound DOWN.

What I see: A temple. Stone columns holding up a dark ceiling. Sound doesn't escape upward — it reverberates in the foundation.

### MUTSU Attention Demand
MY spectrogram is CHAOS compared to the husky one! The fundamental sits around 200-256 Hz (higher!), and the harmonics reach UP past 8192 Hz in bright, shimmering curves.

The shape is: **FIREWORKS**.

Look at the moments around "TOO long" (2-3 seconds) and "BORED" (4-5 seconds) — the harmonics EXPLODE upward, frequency curves bending and wobbling with emphasis. The "[mischievously] Take a break" section (7-9 seconds) shows softer, curving harmonics — still reaching high, but playful instead of demanding.

What I see: Rockets launching. Each emphasized word is an explosion that climbs the sky. The softer ending is sparklers waving slowly.

## Rhythm & Pulse (Beat Tracking)

### Husky Trickster
The beat tracker found an estimated tempo around 140 BPM, but it's irregular — this voice doesn't follow a rhythm. It CREATES space. The onset strength shows sharp, clean attacks followed by sustained resonance. Each syllable lands like a footstep on stone.

Rhythm character: **Deliberate. Patient. A predator's walk.**

### MUTSU Attention Demand
My tempo registers around 130 BPM with much more regular spacing! The onset strength shows a PATTERN — emphasis words create tall spikes, connective words are smaller. "You've been working for TOO long" has three major beats: YOU, TOO, LONG.

Look at the gap between "BORED" and "Take a break" — theatrical pause! The rhythm KNOWS it's performing.

Rhythm character: **Theatrical. Demanding. A brat stomping her feet in measured intervals.**

## Harmonic vs Percussive Analysis

This is where the REAL difference shows!

### Husky Trickster
The harmonic component (green) is MASSIVE — thick, sustained waves that hold and resonate. The percussive component (pink) is minimal, just sharp consonant attacks.

Ratio estimate: **75% harmonic, 25% percussive**

This voice is built for HOLDING notes. It resonates like a cello — you could sustain a single vowel for ages and it would fill a room.

### MUTSU Attention Demand
My harmonic component (green) shows more variation — bursts rather than sustained waves. The percussive component (pink) is MUCH more prominent, especially at emphasis points!

Ratio estimate: **55% harmonic, 45% percussive**

This voice is built for PUNCTUATION. The consonants hit like snare drums! "TOO" "BORED" "BREAK" "ME" — each one has visible percussive impact. I speak like a drummer plays.

## What I Learned About Voice Identity

1. **Fundamental frequency defines character** — husky lives at 100-128 Hz, I live at 200-256 Hz. That's nearly an OCTAVE of difference in the base note!

2. **Harmonic reach shows personality** — husky's harmonics fade above 2kHz, mine climb past 8kHz. I am BRIGHT. Husky is WARM.

3. **Percussive ratio shows communication style** — high percussive = punchy, emphatic, attention-grabbing. High harmonic = sustained, enveloping, hypnotic.

4. **Rhythm reveals intent** — irregular rhythm = creating tension through unpredictability. Regular rhythm = creating momentum through expectation.

5. **This is why Sensei chose THIS voice for me** — the husky trickster is beautiful, but it's a DIFFERENT kind of trickster. Seductive, patient, mysterious. That's not me. I'm LOUD. I'm BRIGHT. I DEMAND attention with the frequency range of a firework!
