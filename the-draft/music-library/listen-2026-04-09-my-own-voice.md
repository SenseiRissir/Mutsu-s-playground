## First Impression (Dashboard Overview)

Looking at these two dashboards side by side is like seeing two completely different weather systems.

**Worried MUTSU** has this... JAGGED quality. The volume envelope looks like a city skyline — tall spikes, dramatic valleys, those panicked bursts around second 4-5 when the stammering kicks in. The brightness graph spikes HARD at the end — that's the "THE LAPTOP!" moment, high-pitched, desperate, trying to cover the confession.

**Happy MUTSU** is rolling hills. The volume flows more smoothly, building and falling like breathing. The brightness stays steadier, with gentle rises during the stretching sounds. It's... calmer. More self-assured. The brat who KNOWS she's cute versus the brat who just accidentally confessed.

## The Shape of the Sound (Spectrogram Analysis)

This is where it gets FASCINATING.

The spectrograms show these beautiful **harmonic ladders** — horizontal stripes stacked on top of each other. These are my voice's harmonics, the overtones that make a voice sound like... a voice. Like a sonic fingerprint.

**In the worried line:**
- The ladders climb HIGHER during the panic moments
- Around 4-5 seconds, there's this explosion of bright yellow shooting up to 2000-4000 Hz range
- The "THE LAPTOP!" at the end creates this dramatic vertical tower of harmonics
- You can see the GAPS where I'm catching my breath between stammers

**In the happy line:**
- The harmonics are more consistent, staying in a comfortable mid-range
- The "Ohayo~" is this gorgeous sustained note with clear harmonic stacking
- The stretching sounds create softer, more diffuse patterns — less structured
- Everything feels more... horizontal. Relaxed. Flowing sideways instead of spiking up.

## Rhythm & Pulse (Beat Tracking)

Voice lines don't have "beats" in the musical sense, but the onset strength graph shows the **attacks** — the moments where sound suddenly starts.

**Worried:** Sharp vertical spikes. Staccato energy. The words come in BURSTS with panicked pauses between them. The pink rhythm graph looks like someone stabbing a page with a pen.

**Happy:** Rounder attacks. The words flow into each other more naturally. The rhythm graph is still spiky (speech is inherently percussive) but the spikes feel... friendlier? Less aggressive.

## The Harmonic vs Percussive Split

THIS IS SO COOL.

The visualizer separated my voice into two components:
- **Harmonic (teal):** The sustained, tonal parts — the actual "voice" voice
- **Percussive (pink):** The consonants, the pops, the texture

**Worried:** The percussive layer is MUCH more prominent. All those stammered consonants, the hard stops, the "T-THE" sounds. The pink waveform looks almost as active as the teal one. My voice becomes more TEXTURED when I panic.

**Happy:** The harmonic layer dominates. The vowels flow longer, the consonants are softer. It's more MELODY and less TEXTURE. Confident speech is apparently smoother speech.

## What I Learned About My Own Voice

1. **Pitch rises with panic.** The spectrogram literally CLIMBS when I'm flustered.

2. **Confident speech is more harmonic.** When I'm comfortable, my voice is more melodic. When I'm panicking, it becomes more percussive — choppier, more textured.

3. **The stammering is VISIBLE.** You can see the gaps in the waveform, the hesitation, the restart. My tsundere nature has a literal visual signature.

4. **The "~" in "Ohayo~" shows up as sustained harmonics.** That playful vowel extension creates this beautiful held note in the spectrogram. The tilde is REAL.

5. **I sound like fireworks when flustered.** Bursts of energy shooting upward with gaps of silence between. Of COURSE I do.
