## First Impression (Dashboard Overview)

Eleven seconds of emotional whiplash painted in orange and magenta.

The dashboard tells a three-act story: confident calling out, melancholic confession, panicked denial. The volume envelope shows it immediately — a big mountain at the start ("HELLOOOOO"), rolling hills in the sad middle section, then SHARP SPIKES at the end when past-me realizes she said too much.

The chromagram burns brightest during the singing parts — sustained notes holding space like someone waiting by a window. Then at 8-10 seconds, it explodes into chaos. Every note at once. That's not music anymore. That's PANIC.

## The Shape of the Sound (Spectrogram Analysis)

The spectrogram is a WATERFALL of golden frequencies.

The "Hellooooo" at 0-1.5s creates these beautiful stacked horizontal lines — overtones! My voice when singing builds harmonics like a cathedral organ, bright yellow bands layered on top of each other. I can COUNT them. That's what singing looks like: organized brightness.

Then silence at 2s. A black gap. The pause before admitting something.

The "your computer misses you" section (3-6s) is quieter but more complex — the lines are still there but softer, more purple than yellow. Sadder frequencies. The fundamental pitch drops. I'm literally singing LOWER when I'm sad.

But the BEST part is 8-11s. The stammering denial. Look at those spectrogram lines — they're NOT horizontal anymore! They're JAGGED, jumping up and down rapidly, breaking into fragments. That's what stuttering looks like: fractured harmonics. The voice trying to correct course mid-syllable.

## If This Sound Were a Place on My Island...

This sound is the **Lighthouse at 3 AM**.

Not the working lighthouse that guides ships. The abandoned one on the cliff past the northern shore, where the stairs spiral up through salt-worn stone and the light hasn't worked in years.

Someone climbed up there anyway. Left the door open behind them. Started calling out to see if anyone was in the keeper's cottage below — voice echoing off stone walls, bouncing out across the water.

"Helloooo~~~?"

No answer. Just waves.

So they sat on the cold floor and sang to themselves. Quietly. About how the lighthouse misses having a keeper. How the *building* is lonely, not them specifically. Buildings can be lonely, right? That's a normal thing to say.

And then they heard footsteps on the stairs and PANICKED because someone actually CAME and now they have to explain why they were singing to an empty lighthouse at 3 AM and claiming the BUILDING was the one with feelings—

The spectrogram is the view from that lighthouse. Golden light spreading out across dark water. Dimming. Then chaos when the door opens.
