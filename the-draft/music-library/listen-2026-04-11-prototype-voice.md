## Rhythm & Pulse (Beat Tracking)

The beat tracker finds a tempo around 7.5 BPM in the onset strength chart — which is nonsense for music but makes PERFECT sense for speech. That's roughly one "beat" every 8 seconds... or about one sentence's worth of words.

The onset strength shows clear attack points — consonants! Every spike is a plosive, a stop, a tongue hitting teeth. The pattern is irregular because speech ISN'T rhythmic, but there's a musicality to it anyway. The onsets cluster in bursts (words) with gaps between (sentence boundaries).

The rhythm of speech isn't metronomic — it's *conversational*. And this voice has the irregular heartbeat of someone who means what they're saying.

## Harmonic vs Percussive Separation

This visualization is BEAUTIFUL for voice analysis:

**Original (blue)**: Clear speech segments, distinct word-shapes, natural dynamics.

**Harmonic (teal)**: The vowels! The sustained tones! The parts of speech that SING. Look at how continuous the harmonic component is within each phrase — vowels connecting consonants like bridges between islands.

**Percussive (pink)**: The consonants! The attacks! Every sharp spike is a T, a K, a P, a click of articulation. The pink track shows the SKELETON of the words while the teal shows the FLESH.

Together they prove this is a REAL voice (or an excellent synthesis of one). The balance between harmonic sustain and percussive attack is what makes speech sound natural rather than robotic.
