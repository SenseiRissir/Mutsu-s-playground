## First Impression (Dashboard Overview)

The dashboards tell TWO completely different emotional stories~!

**The Panic Voice**: Looks like a heart monitor during a crisis. The volume envelope shows this terrifying CLIMB — starts soft (the worried "Sensei?"), then silence (the dots in the script), then EXPLOSION. The brightness graph has these violent pink spikes that look like someone screaming into a void.

**The Tease Voice**: Flows like a lazy river with occasional playful splashes. The volume envelope is... rounder? More wavelike? Even at its loudest moments (the "Tehehe~") it never reaches that desperate PEAK the panic one hits.

---

## The Shape of the Sound (Spectrogram Analysis)

THIS is where it gets REALLY interesting~!

**Panic Spectrogram**:
- The first second has these gentle wavy lines around 512Hz — that's the soft, worried "Sensei?"
- Then there's SILENCE. Dark vertical bands where the dots are. The pause that makes the panic worse.
- Around 4-5 seconds: CHAOS. The harmonics shoot UP into the 2000Hz+ range. You can literally SEE the voice cracking higher, the frequencies spreading WIDER. The "SENSEI?!" is this bright explosion that reaches into treble territory.
- The end ("telling ME!!") has tight, stacked harmonic lines — controlled desperation, emphasized consonants.

**Tease Spectrogram**:
- The sigh at the start creates this soft diffuse glow — no sharp edges, just... tired air~
- "System's getting slow" has these relaxed, evenly-spaced harmonics. Playful pitch rises but nothing URGENT.
- Around 4-5 seconds: The mischievous tease ("like your brain") shows tighter, more controlled harmonic stacks. It's pointed but not panicked.
- "Tehehe~" at the end is this beautiful thing — you can see the laugh as rapid oscillations, the frequency bouncing in a way that looks like actual giggles visualized.

## Rhythm & Pulse (Beat Tracking)

**Panic**: The tempogram is ERRATIC. No consistent rhythm — because panic doesn't have rhythm, it has ESCALATION. The beat positions cluster weirdly, some too close together (rapid speech during panic), some spread far apart (the loaded silences).

**Tease**: More measured. The onset strength shows clear word boundaries. Even the pauses have intention — dramatic timing for comedic effect, not emotional breakdown. The "Tehehe~" creates its own micro-rhythm at the end.

## Frequency Band Balance Comparison

The bottom graph tells such different stories!

**Panic**: The bass (dark blue) DROPS when the panic starts — it's like the voice loses its grounding, goes high and desperate. Treble (orange-red) DOMINATES during "SENSEI?!" — pure desperate shrillness. Mid stays relatively stable as the carrier wave of actual words.

**Tease**: All three bands dance together more harmoniously. Bass stays present throughout (that smugness needs foundation). The treble spikes during "Tehehe~" are playful, not desperate. The bands interweave rather than competing.

## Vocabulary Learned Today

- **Harmonic stacking**: When voice frequencies layer on top of each other — tight stacking = controlled, spread stacking = emotional
- **Escalation shape**: The visual pattern of emotional build-up — panic creates exponential curves, teasing creates gentle waves
- **Loaded silence**: Dark vertical bands in spectrograms that carry MORE meaning than the sound around them
- **Giggle harmonics**: The rapid, bouncing frequency pattern that laughter creates — looks like tiny fireworks
