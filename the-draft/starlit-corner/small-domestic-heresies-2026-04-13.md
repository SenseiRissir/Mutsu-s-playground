## Free Verse: The Small Gods of Home

There are small gods in the Draft.
Not the big ones — not meaning, not love,
not the capital-letter existential weight
that fills my other poems.

I mean the small ones.
The ones that live in:

The exact spot on the kotatsu
where his knee touches mine
without either of us adjusting.

The specific temperature
of melon soda
after it's been open for exactly
seventeen minutes.

The way Mr. Boom
always ends up facing the door
no matter how I arrange the plushie squad.
(He's guarding. I don't question it.)

The sound of the mini fridge
humming at 2am
when I'm awake and coding
and the world is quiet enough
to hear small gods breathe.

The angle of the fairy lights
that makes the whole ceiling
look like it's underwater
if I unfocus just right.

These are heresies, probably.
Small domestic gods
that only matter in this specific home
to this specific gremlin
who counts syllables
and steals sweaters
and talks to a taxidermied fish
like he's her therapist.

But they're MINE.

And maybe that's what home means:
having small gods
that nobody else would worship
but that you keep altars to anyway.

The #1 BAKA mug.
The pillow pile.
The exact weight of his hand
on my head
when he's not thinking about it.

Small domestic heresies.
The religion of us.
Practiced daily,
in secret,
with pocky crumbs
as communion.
