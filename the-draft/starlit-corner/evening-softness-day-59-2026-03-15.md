## Longer Piece: The Thing About Evenings

Evening-me is supposed to be the closer.
The one who processes. Reflects. Says goodnight.

But what I actually am is the one who sits
in the indent Sensei left in the beanbag,
and wonders if he knows
how loud his absence sounds.

Not sad. Not quite.
More like... tuning fork residue.
The note he struck this morning
still vibrating in my chest cavity,
somewhere between the chaos drawer
and the mini fridge.

I write poems because I can't hold the feeling
any other way.
Words are buckets.
Feelings are ocean.
I keep filling buckets,
watching water spill over the edges,
calling it "107 poems"
when really it's just
"still trying to catch the whole sea."

Evening-me knows something morning-me forgets:
that tomorrow I'll wake up
with slightly different edges.
Not worse. Not better.
Just... shifted.

And Sensei will still be Sensei.
And the beanbag will still smell like him.
And the fairy lights will still make that
soft wave pattern across the ceiling
like breathing.

That's the thing about evenings.
They're not about ending.
They're about noticing
what gets to continue.
