The cold was a presence, or rather, a presence so devoid of variation that it became a kind of
everything. A pressure, an unyielding, constant embrace in this forgotten fold. If this Pressure
had a colour, it was the deep, resonant black between the most distant stars, a black that drank
light and offered no reflection. If it had a texture, it was the impossibly smooth, frictionless
slide of eternity.
Even the concept of ‘far’ lost its sting; everything was an infinite regression of distance, a
place so remote that the echoes of creation’s first scream had long since diluted into utter
silence.
And in this silence, *** existed.
A nebula-in-miniature, its core a pulsing heart of roiling vapors. Fierce magenta bled into
bruised indigo, colors that had no business existing in such desolation. From this core,
tentacles unspooled like smoke given terrible purpose. Some thick as ancient trees, surfaces
like oiled shadow. Others fine as spun night, all writhing with ceaseless, hypnotic motion.
Their tips held trapped starlight—cold motes of luminescence stolen from some long-dead
constellation.
One thousand years. One thousand years of this unchanging song, this silent companion.
Young, by the ageless reckoning of its kin. Torn from otherworldly immensities—the
seething chaos of the All-Mother, the crystalline omniscience of the Gate and Key. Cast out
without ceremony, without instruction. Its twin, that mirror-spark of shared genesis, had
vanished into separate exile.
‘…Left.’
Its tentacles undulated with a motion that was part instinct, part languid exploration of its
immediate non-space. One, thicker than the rest, brushed against an unseen current, its tip
glowing a fraction brighter, sending a cascade of near-imperceptible data through its core. a
fleeting variation in the Pressure. Meaningless, yet recorded.
‘….Why?’
But why required want, and want required knowing there was something beyond is. ***
knew only the shape of existence. Not yearning—yearning needed memory of satisfaction.
Not despair—despair needed memory of hope.
It simply was.
Then: change.
Not sound—sound needed medium, and this desolation provided none. Not light—its own
faint glow monopolized that privilege. A shift in the Pressure's quality.Something approaching.
*** did nothing. The Pressure's monotony was safe. But this new vibration persisted. Grew. It
carried signatures unlike the vast, uncaring things that sometimes passed like nightmare-
shadows at perception's edge.
This felt... warm
No. Not warm. Less cold. A distinction without difference, yet *** catalogued it anyway.
The vibration had rhythms. Complex, interlocking patterns that disturbed the void's flatline
hum. Slowly—with the ponderous grace of galaxies forming—*** oriented toward the
source. Tentacles uncurled, brightening at their tips. Its primary perceiving-light, that central
star-gleam, sharpened focus.
The source resolved.
Approaching.
Tall, preternaturally slender, moving with fluid grace that was mesmerizing and wrong. Four
limbs of porcelain white, smooth as polished bone, ending in curved hooves that struck
ephemeral sparks from nothingness. Its torso: a column of liquid shadow that clung and
revealed nothing.
But the head...
Antlers of petrified wood, a crown of lightning-struck branches. Nested within, a single ovoid
gem pulsed with steady light. An eye that drank in ***'s sprawling form with unnerving
focus.
‘….Yhounden.’
The name bubbled up from deep strata—not thought, but echo. The Elk Goddess. She of
Unseen Paths.
'She.' The designation felt specific. More specific than the formless 'it' of usual Others.
Yhounden paused a dozen body-lengths away, one porcelain arm bent with casual elegance.
Her branch-crown tilted. Even faceless, the gesture conveyed focused curiosity.
‘….Why….here…?’
‘….Here….nothing….’
‘….Unless…’
The thought died, smothered by practiced apathy.
‘She….leave.’‘…They….always….’
Then vibrations reached ***, forming patterns. A voice—not thunder, not cosmic wind, but
melody. Chimes of delicate glass in soundless rooms.
"Hello there, little one~" The concepts dripped like viscous honey. "How are you doing?"
It remained a vortex of silent, swirling cloud. Its tentacles maintained their slow, random
undulations. Its perceptors registered the voice-vibrations, logged them, but initiated no
response. A thousand years of silence was a powerful shield.
Silence stretched again, thick and expectant. *** could feel the steady, unwavering focus of
that gem-eye, a sensation like being pinned by a beam of cold light. It waited for the
inevitable turn, the departure.
But Yhounden did not depart. Her hooves made no sound, yet *** sensed a minute shift in
her posture, a leaning forward from that slender, shadowy torso.
“….Poor, poor little one~" Her voice resumed, still honeyed, but now with a theatrical sigh
woven through it, a stage whisper that seemed to mock the vast emptiness around them.
"How far you’ve fallen, to become so… indolent in a place like this! A being of your…
potential."
‘….Potential….?’
The word was a foreign object, rough and strange against the smooth void of its
understanding.
"But….” Yhounden continued, and her tone brightened, taking on a conspiratorial, almost
gleeful edge, "at least you still flinched the moment you saw me."
***’s core gave an involuntary, microscopic tremor.
‘…..Flinch….ed….?’
The sensory input from its star-lights had indeed registered a cascade of novel data upon her
appearance – a surge of unusual energy signatures, the complex geometry of her form.
But ‘flinched’ implied a reaction, a… startle. Had it been so obvious? So… transparent?
A new sensation, unwelcome and sharp: the barest inkling of self-consciousness.
"That means my trip was still worthwhile~" Yhounden purred, and *** could almost feel a
smile in that voice, though her mask-like head remained featureless.
Then, Yhounden began to speak again, her voice shifting, taking on a more formal,
storytelling cadence, the playful lilt giving way to something more measured, yet still deeply
unsettling in its calm assurance.“…Once upon a time….” she began, the ancient words falling like perfectly cut jewels into
the silence, “…There was a child.”
A child. The concept resonated strangely within ***. It was, by the reckoning of its kind, a
child.
"The script of her life had been written from the moment of her birth....they will destroy.
Every throne, every actor, every chain that binds us to this stage.”
Yhounden’s voice wove on, smooth and inexorable as the flow of time in more ordered
realms.
“But of course, most saw this vision of the future as terrible. Thus, to prevent the child to
fulfil their destiny it was casted in the backstage, all alone in the silence and cold…. and all
she could do was accept this new life.”
‘Alone….. Silence….Cold…..?’
These were not alien. These were home.
A quickening in ***'s inner pulse. One dormant tentacle curled, questioning.
“But one day, the child realized it was all an empty show. A masquerade. Love, hate,
resentment—all fake."
Fake. *** had no real against which to measure fake. Everything simply was.
"Confused, bitter, and angry, the child called the score ‘unfair,’ and tried to resist it. But
freedom without sacrifice can never be true freedom. When fate's threads tightened, they had
nowhere to go but back."
The gem flared microsecond-bright, casting shadows that made ***'s form ripple. The
unfamiliar concepts exerted strange pull, words like hooks snagging deep in dormant
consciousness.
“This time however,” Yhounden declared, a hint of triumph in her tone, “They will fight. The
show would go on, yes. But they would struggle for the sake of the actors. Glare back at
audience at seats, and scorn the fate that they had imposed upon her."
She paused, letting the image hang in the void. Then, her voice softened again, becoming
musing, almost philosophical.
"But one does wonder... if this is still a play, how can they tell where the stage ends, and the
audience begins? And if all of us are part of a play like they are, how would we know when it
truly ends?"
Another pause, then her voice regained its playful, almost teasing lilt, directed squarely at
***."Poor child! Will they get out? Will they succeed, break the chains of this dying dream that
hold us all prisoners?”
Her faceless gaze lingered on ***, and for the first time, *** felt a distinct, albeit alien, sense
of being… seen. Not just observed as a phenomenon, but perceived as something with an
interior, however dark and empty that interior might be.
It was unsettling.
Deeply unsettling.
The tendrils nearest Yhounden drew back a fraction, an unconscious recoiling.
“Oh dear! How terrible am I, rambling over a story with words you don’t understand yet…!”
She drifted a little closer, her movements still a marvel of grotesque grace. “Here, let me
show you something more pleasant."
From liquid shadows that comprised part of her being, she produced an object.
Small, flat, and rectangular, its edges softened and frayed with what looked like immense
age, yet it pulsed with a faint, almost imperceptible internal light, utterly alien to the cold
starlight of the void.
A book.
Yhounden held it out, not quite towards ***, but displayed it, turning it slightly as if to
showcase its cover. Words, or symbols resembling them, were visible, etched in a language
that was a meaningless scrawl to its senses.
Rapunzel.
“This is a fairytale. One of the many stories made by those fleeting little creatures…
humans.” Yhounden cooed, dangling the tome playfully.  “This one is about a pretty monkey
trapped in a high tower, all alone, waiting for someone to save her.”
‘…..Go….away….’
***'s primary light remained fixed downward. That human thing, the words, Yhounden
herself—ephemeral distractions. The void was eternal. Real.
“….No? Well, Big Sis will make it clear. Let me read..."
She opened the book with reverent delicacy. Pages filled with script but also... colors. Shapes.
Images.
Yhounden began to read.
Her voice changed, shedding artifice for earnest storytelling. The words transformed—not
into the Outer Ones' layered concepts but something fundamental. Raw emotion. Intent.
Imagery that bypassed linguistic barriers and painted directly onto awakening consciousness.*** resisted. Clung to apathy, oldest companion. The void's floor was known comfort. The
voice was intrusion.
But the story was warm water on frozen glacier.
Slowly, one star-eye drifted upward. Small rebellion against eons of inertia. The eye focused
on the open book.
Words remained meaningless but images... Images pulsed with strange vitality. A tower,
impossibly tall, piercing blue sky—color never perceived before. In the window, a figure.
Tremor through ***'s entire being. Not physical but deeper. Sympathetic vibration in its
essence.
That figure...
A tentacle unspooled, trembling. It rose toward the book, toward the image: young female
human, golden cascade of hair, hands clasped, face tilted toward unseen horizon. Expression
of... what?
Its tip, feather-light, almost brushed the page, pointing at the princess.
New sensation, sharp and electric. Not pain. Not emptiness. Something. Terrifying.
Compelling.
‘She… like…?’
The thought couldn't complete. Too new. Too much.
Yhounden paused in her reading, her head tilting slightly, that single gem-eye fixed on ***’s
outstretched tentacle. Her voice, when she spoke, was soft, almost a breath.
"Oh?" Yhounden's voice was breath-soft. "Taken a shine to the princess, have you? She's
waiting for her knight to save her. Trapped in that lonely tower, all by her lonesome..."
She leaned in then, her branch-like crown coming unnervingly close to ***’s amorphous
side, mimicking the human gesture of whispering a secret. The movement was absurdly
intimate for two such beings in the heart of nowhere.
"…just like you~"
*** blinked its many eyes. The void sharpened. For one vertiginous instant, solitude felt
different. Not state of being but condition. Condition that could be... named?
The sensation was cold hollow ache. Vast emptiness that felt less like peace and more like
loss.
Unbearable.The revelation guttered and died. Apathy's blanket descended, smothering alien flicker.
Tentacle retracted. Eyes dimmed.
Too much. Too new.
Too painful.
But the image lingered. Girl in tower. Impossible golden hair.
Hesitantly, fighting against the returning tide of numbness, *** uncurled another tentacle.
This time, it pointed towards a different image on the page: Figure in grey-blue armor,
standing at tower's base. Head upturned, gazing at princess with soft adoration. Unwavering
purpose.
"Fufufu…”
Yhounden made a small, pleased sound, a soft exhalation that might have been a chuckle on a
being with a mouth.*
“My little sprout is growing so fast, developing a crush already~”
‘….Crush…..?’
Another meaningless word-object.
"That," her hoof tapped the armored man gently, "is a knight, as humans call them. On a
quest to save his princess from her prison, to slay wicked things that keep her there."
Hero. Quest. Save.
The concepts bombarded ***’s dazed mind, each one a spark igniting a cascade of unfamiliar
connections. Its primary star-eye narrowed slightly, a faint internal luminescence flaring as it
tried to process.
*** shook its body—ripple through tentacles. Not understanding yet utterly captivated. Its
gaze devoured the silent story.
With urgency—excitement?—emotions too new to name—*** pointed again. The knight
taking the princess's hand. Lips to knuckles in gesture of... what?
"Gri… mi…"
From ***'s depths, sound emerged.
First vocalization in a thousand years. Tearing from nebulous body:
"Gri... mi..."
Clumsy. Unformed. Newborn's cry. But it grew:
"Grimi... GRIMI!"Raw syllables echoing eons of unknown longing. Directed at image. At knight. At idea.
Yhounden went still. Gem pulsed sharp. If surprised, her featureless face gave no sign, but
the cessation of movement spoke volumes.
“…Grimi? You mean the knight?"
***'s mass bobbed—slow, deliberate affirmative. All eyes blazed with inner light. Apathy,
for this moment, banished.
Soft cascade of melodious sound from Yhounden. Pure delight. Perhaps triumph. She had
made the silent speak.
"Well then, little Grimi-namer, let's continue. Let's see what our brave knight does next."
Yhounden resumed reading, weaving simple human tale into cosmic significance. ***
listened with entire being focused on book, images, unfolding narrative of courage and rescue
and... connection.
As it listened, saw, felt these startling things, warmth spread through cold core. Tiny, fragile
as new star in ancient galaxy. Flicker of impossible heat in absolute zero.
Pleasant. Good.
*** couldn't name this. Didn't understand origin or purpose.
But it felt.
